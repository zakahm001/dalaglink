📚 Soil & Fertilizer Guide

Sourced from FAO SWALIM, FAO BRiMS project, and peer-reviewed agronomy research — not generic advice. Adapted specifically for Somalia's soil and water conditions.

🌊 Soil Salinity — The #1 Issue in Shabelle & Jubba

Soil fertility is actively declining in irrigated floodplain areas due to over-irrigation and salinization. This is the single most damaging long-term issue along the rivers.

Source: Water Use Efficiency study, Afgoi, Somalia — 2026; FAO SWALIM Land Degradation report

What it looks like: stunted growth, leaf-tip burning, poor germination, white crust on dry soil surface — even when soil appears wet, salt-stressed crops behave as if drought-stressed.

What farmers can do (low-cost, practical):

  • Flush fields with extra fresh water before planting to push salts below the root zone
  • Avoid letting fields sit flooded for long periods — standing water concentrates salt as it evaporates
  • Favor salt-tolerant crops on the worst plots: sorghum and cowpea tolerate salinity far better than maize or vegetables
  • Improve drainage — even simple channels reduce waterlogging, the main driver of salinization

🗺️ Somalia's Soil Types

Shabelle & Jubba floodplains: clayey soils, some with poor drainage and high salt content — flood-prone

Central coastal: sandy soils along the coast

Central inland: moderately deep loamy soils, high calcium carbonate/gypsum content

Inter-riverine / NW border areas: shallow soils, particularly toward Ethiopia

Source: FAO SWALIM — Soils of Somalia

🚰 Irrigation Infrastructure Reality

All eight major barrages on the Shabelle river are non-functional since the 1990s collapse; canals are heavily silted. Most irrigating farmers rely on diesel pumps drawing directly from the river — expensive and unreliable.

Source: Basnyat 2007; Afgoi field study 2026

Why drip irrigation matters here: in comparable conditions (Ethiopia), drip-irrigated tomato achieved 4.2–5.8 kg fruit per cubic meter of water — multiples better than flood irrigation. It also reduces diesel cost for pump-dependent farmers and reduces salinity buildup versus flooding.

Source: Wondatir & Belay, 2020
⚠️ Key Insight

Somalia's average fertilizer use is just 4 kg/ha — far below regional standards. This means most farmers are starting from near-zero, not over-fertilizing. Even small, correctly-timed applications produce large yield jumps.

🍅 Tomato Fertilization

Needs Nitrogen, Phosphorus (P2O5) and Potassium (K2O). On soils testing low in P and K: up to 150 lb P2O5 and 200 lb K2O per acre is appropriate — half that on medium-fertility soil.

Timing: Phosphorus should go in before planting or at transplanting — it doesn't move through soil easily. Potassium can go in before planting, as side-dressing, or fed through drip irrigation during growth.

Source: UC IPM Tomato Fertilization Guidelines

🌽 Maize Fertilization

Split nitrogen applications (smaller amounts, multiple times) consistently outperform one large application — even more so under drip irrigation. Basal fertilizer placed near the root zone 1 week after sowing performs better than broadcasting on the surface.

Source: Nigeria Middle-Belt maize study, 2024; Inner Mongolia drip-fertigation study, 2025
